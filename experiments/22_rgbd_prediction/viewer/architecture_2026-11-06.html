Single-frame SQ predictor (exp 22)

RGB-D frame → set of plain superquadrics in the camera frame, each with existence, class and instance mask. Two models: dense (--dense, model.DenseModel), one SQ per patch trained one-to-many, and top-k (--topk, model.TopKModel), the dense model as encoder plus a decoder over its 300 best proposals trained one-to-one. State of code/model.py, code/dense.py and code/train.py on 2026-11-06.

Status

Both models fit one frame and 16 frames. The heatmap existence fixed the selection problem of the earlier patch-query models: on 16 frames top-k finds 85 % of the visible SQs at existence 0.3 (the old patch-query model 50 %). On the full training set (7 scenes, 1400 frames) they still fit the training frames moderately, but do not generalize to room_0 at all. For dense, 34 % of the SQs of training frames have a proposal with IoU > 0.5, against 1.4 % on room_0. The open problem is therefore generalization (data, targets, inputs) more than optimization.

Data & targets

The 8 NICE-SLAM Replica scenes, every 10th rendered frame, 200 frames per scene, cached by cache_frames.py / cache_sonata.py in Thesis/data/replica_rgbd_frames. Training uses 7 scenes (1400 frames); testing uses room_0 (200 frames).

The cache stores a frame's targets as every SQ of its scene's target decomposition (results/targets_winding: robust SuperFlex + post-optimization per GT instance, plain SQs) that touches the camera frustum, in the camera frame (x right, y down, z forward). Both models train and evaluate only on the visible SQs among them: SQs that own at least one pixel (see assignment). Hidden SQs are dropped, which is about 30 % of the cached targets.

Frozen features (cached per frame)

DINOv3 ViT-L/16 frozen

facebook/dinov3-vitl16-pretrain-lvd1689m, ImageNet normalization, run in bfloat16. Last-layer patch tokens of rows 4..676 of the 1200×680 image: 42×75 × 1024, stored as float16. No augmentation, so the tokens are identical every epoch.

Sonata (PTv3) frozen

Input: the frame's valid depth pixels at /4 (168×300), back-projected and moved to the world frame (z up, as Sonata was trained), with color and a normal from the depth map, at 2 cm grid sampling. The 3 coarsest stages are kept (192 / 384 / 512 channels; roughly 8 / 16 / 32 cm voxels), stored as stage tokens plus the token index of every pixel per stage. Per pixel that gives 1088 channels.

Neither feature set has been inspected visually yet; this is the first viewer of experiment 23.

One-to-many assignment

dense.assign, per frame, without any prediction-dependent cost:

  1. Pixel owner (train.pixel_owner): each labelled pixel is back-projected; among the target SQs of its GT instance it belongs to the one with the smallest SDF at that point. Pixels of instances without target SQs and unlabelled pixels have no owner.
  2. Patch owner: a patch is positive for the SQ that owns ≥ 50 % of its 16 pixels; otherwise it is a negative.
  3. Thin parts: a visible SQ without any such patch gets the patch where its share is largest (about 10 SQs per frame).
  4. Visible SQs = SQs that own at least one pixel. Only these are targets, for the proposals and for the decoder.

Typical frame: about 2,900 of 3,150 patches are positive; an SQ owns between 1 and about 1,000 patches (median 33).

Existence heatmap and selection

A per-patch "lies on an SQ" target is useless for ranking: in the first dense overfit 99.5 % of patches scored ≈ 0.995, and the top 300 all came from one SQ. So existence is trained CenterNet style (dense.heatmap, dense.center_focal):

  • per SQ, its center = the patch deepest inside its region (largest distance to the region's border, distance_transform_edt);
  • target = a Gaussian around the center with σ = max(0.5, √(patches) / 4) in patch units, restricted to the SQ's own patches; 1 at the center; heatmaps of different SQs are combined by max;
  • loss = penalty-reduced focal loss (α 2, β 4): −(1−p)² log p at centers, −(1−h)⁴ p² log(1−p) elsewhere, summed and divided by the number of centers.

Selection (model.peaks): a patch is kept only if it is the maximum of its 3×3 neighbourhood (max-pool NMS); all others are set to −1. Proposals are the top K = 300 of what remains, ranked by existence. When there are fewer than 300 local maxima, the rest are filled with arbitrary non-maxima (score −1).

exist (B, 42·75) ──max_pool 3×3──▶ keep where exist == pooled ──▶ topk(300)

Dense (proposal) loss

dense.losses, averaged over the frames of a batch. Up to 512 positive patches per frame are sampled without replacement with probability ∝ 1 / (patches of their SQ), so that every SQ counts about equally regardless of its size. Each sampled patch is compared with the SQ that owns it:

termweightdefinition
exist2CenterNet focal loss over all 3150 patches (above)
cd10Chamfer(patch SQ, owner SQ), 8×8 regular (η, ω) surface samples each, sum of both directions (m)
forced10the same with the patch SQ moved to the owner's translation (SuperFlex: shape learns while the position is wrong)
cls2cross entropy of the owner's class
mask / dice5 / 5BCE and Dice of the patch's 42×75 mask against the owner's instance mask (share of the instance's pixels per patch)

Negative patches get only the existence term. The mask target is the instance, not the SQ, so all SQs of one instance learn the same mask.

Top-k decoder --topk · 312 k

model.TopKModel: the dense model above is the encoder and its 3150 per-patch SQs are proposals.

  1. Select the K = 300 proposals as in selection, on the detached existence map. Selection is not differentiable.
  2. Query = the selected patches' encoder output tokens (not detached: the decoder loss trains the encoder too). Reference = their proposal scale, exponents and translation, detached.
  3. Decoder layer (--dec_layers = 1): q ← layer(q + Linear(Fourier(ref.trans)), memory = all 3150 encoder tokens), a pre-norm nn.TransformerDecoderLayer (self-attention over the 300 queries, cross-attention to the memory, FFN 512, 8 heads, GELU, no dropout), then a LayerNorm. The positional term is added into the residual stream once per layer, not to queries and keys inside each attention as in DETR.
  4. Refine heads (RefineHeads): scale = ref · exp(Δ), exponents = 0.1 + 1.8 σ(logit(ref) + Δ), translation = ref + Δ, with all Δ layers initialized to 0, so the decoder starts exactly at the proposals. Rotation, existence, class and mask are predicted anew.
  5. With more layers, each layer's output (detached) becomes the next reference and earlier layers go to aux, each with its own loss. Not used yet.

Top-k loss

dense.topk_losses = the dense loss on the proposals (logged as proposals/*) + the Hungarian loss train.losses on the decoder's 300 SQs against the visible SQs of the frame, weight 1 each:

Training

  • AdamW, lr 1e-4, weight decay 1e-4, OneCycle, gradient clip 1.0, batch 8 frames, no augmentation.
  • 1 frame (--overfit room_2_000500, 68 visible SQs): 3000 steps.
  • 16 frames (--frames 16, evenly spaced over the training scenes, about 42 visible SQs each): 6000 steps, train = evaluation frames.
  • Full: 150 epochs × 175 steps = 26 k steps. Evaluation every 10 epochs on room_0 (200 frames) and 100 training frames.

Evaluation

Proposals (dense, and enc_* for top-k)

  • recall@0.5: share of visible SQs with at least one of the 300 proposals at pair IoU > 0.5; best IoU: mean of that best IoU. Pair IoU = volumetric IoU on 1024 points uniform in the pair's joint box.
  • exist precision / recall: patches with existence > 0.5 against the assigned positive patches. Recall is low by design (only centers are 1).
  • predicted: patches with existence > 0.5 (compare with the number of visible SQs).
  • occupancy IoU: union of the predicted SQs (top 300, or existence > 0.5) against the union of the visible SQs, 32 k points in their box.
  • class accuracy of the positive patches.

Decoder (top-k)

  • Hungarian matching with the training cost to the visible SQs. pair IoU and Chamfer of the matched pairs (independent of existence).
  • found @0.5 / @0.3: share of visible SQs whose matched query is above the threshold; predicted @0.5 / @0.3.
  • occupancy IoU @0.5 / @0.3; mask coverage: queries above 0.5 grouped by mask overlap into instances, mean best IoU per GT instance.

Where it may go wrong

Ranked by how well they fit the evidence: both models fit 16 frames, fit 1400 training frames moderately, and fail on room_0.

1Too little data for generalization

7 scenes, each seen from 200 nearby viewpoints, no augmentation, frozen features identical every epoch. Class accuracy is 0.99 on training frames, so the model can identify scene and position and read off the answer. room_0 is also the densest scene (68 visible SQs against about 41).

Check: leave-one-scene-out on an easier test scene (office_1, room_1); augmentation (flips, crops, depth scale). Fix: more scenes (rest of Replica, ScanNet++, exp 18's chairs).

1Target definition is not learnable as a rule

The targets are a per-instance SQ decomposition fitted offline. The number of SQs per instance and where one SQ ends and the next begins are not determined by the image; neighbouring instances of the same kind can be split differently. Across 1400 frames these inconsistent splits have to be averaged, which fits training frames only moderately and does not transfer.

Check (results viewer): do test predictions find the right objects but split them differently, or miss objects altogether? Compare instance-level metrics (mask coverage, occupancy) against SQ-level ones.

2Existence peaks do not match SQs

The dense model predicts 107–169 peaks above 0.5 for 41–68 visible SQs, so several peaks per SQ (large SQs such as walls have broad Gaussians with flat tops) and the 300 slots fill with duplicates. On room_0 the peaks may also sit on regions that the training scenes never decomposed this way.

Check: the existence map and peaks overlaid on RGB in the results viewer. Fix: smaller σ, or 5×5 NMS, or an IoU-aware score.

2Proposal quality bounds the decoder

The decoder only refines the 300 selected proposals, with one layer. If the right SQ is not among the proposals, it cannot be recovered. On 16 frames, proposal recall is 0.28, yet 85 % are found at 0.3, so the decoder does fix a lot. On room_0 the proposals are already broken (best IoU 0.12).

Fix: more decoder layers with iterative refinement and auxiliary losses (planned, see the earlier thread); learned queries next to the proposals.

3Inputs not verified

Nobody has looked at the DINO and Sonata features of these frames. Possible issues: misaligned crop rows, Sonata in the world frame against camera-frame predictions, pixels mapped to the wrong stage tokens.

Check: the feature viewer of experiment 23 (PCA colors and similarity maps per frame).

3Capacity

d = 128, 1 encoder layer, 1 decoder layer, 1.15 M trainable parameters. The fit of training frames drops from 0.62 (1 frame) to 0.42 (16) to 0.34 (1400) in dense recall, so capacity contributes to the underfitting, but it cannot explain the train / test gap of 0.34 against 0.014.
