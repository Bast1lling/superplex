Single-frame SQ predictor (exp 22)

One RGB-D frame → the plain superquadrics of the visible scene parts, in the camera frame, each with existence, class and instance mask. This page describes only the current pipeline, as of 2026-11-07. Defaults are given first; --flags name the live alternatives.

Pipeline

data target fitting cache / projection frozen backbone transformer prediction heads

1 · Data

  • Replica, the 8 NICE-SLAM scenes. Rendered trajectories give RGB, depth and the GT instance per pixel; there are 51 classes. Every 10th frame is used, 200 per scene.
  • Split. 7 training scenes (room_1, room_2, office_0–4; 1400 frames), test on room_0 (200 frames). --frames 16 --train_scenes 1 overfits on 16 frames (train = eval).
  • View. Rows 4..676 of the 1200 × 680 image. DINO sees 1200 × 672 (42 × 75 patches of 16 px); depth and labels are kept at /4 = 168 × 300 (4 × 4 pixels per patch). Pinhole f = 150, camera frame x right, y down, z forward.

Files: ../12_rgbd_multiview (views, GT grid) · data in Thesis/data/replica_rgbd_frames

2 · Target SQs

  • Per GT instance, independently, as in experiment 16 (--plain): SuperFlex robust as the initialization (tapering and bending off), then 1000 Adam steps of SuperFlex's objective, keeping the best state.
  • Occupancy of the query points: winding number of the instance's own oriented points (occupancy.py). This closes objects without turning the space behind walls into wall.
  • Result. 1216 SQs in the 7 training scenes and 250 in room_0, in the world frame (results/targets_winding/<scene>/sqs.npz).
The decomposition is not consistent across scenes: a floor gets 1–5 SQs, a door 1–5, and a chair has 2.0–9.6 SQs on average depending on the scene. A model cannot learn that as a rule; this is task 3, choosing targets.

Files: code/fit_targets.py, code/occupancy.py

3 · Per-frame cache

cache_frames.py → <frame>_dino.npy, _view.npz:

  • DINOv3 ViT-L/16 last-layer patch tokens (42 × 75 × 1024, fp16, frozen).
  • RGB, depth and GT instance label at 168 × 300.
  • Every target SQ that intersects the camera frustum, transformed into the camera frame (≈ 65 per frame).

cache_assign.py → <frame>_assign.npz (rebuilt 2026-11-07):

  • Pixel owner (train.pixel_owner). Among the SQs of the pixel's GT instance, the first one its ray enters (hard ray casting, render.entry_depths). If the ray misses them all, the one with the smallest |SDF|. The owner's entry depth is stored too (ownz).
  • Visible targets. SQs owning ≥ 1 pixel, ≈ 44 per frame. Only these are training targets.
  • Patch owner. The SQ owning ≥ 50 % of a patch's pixels, else negative. Every visible SQ without such a patch gets the patch where its share is largest.
  • Heatmap (CenterNet). One Gaussian per SQ over its own patches, peaking at its innermost patch, σ = max(0.5, √patches / 4).
  • f = visible / camera-facing surface area of each target (train.visible_fraction), for both samplings (f, f_cube). A surface sample is visible if all of these hold:
    • it lies in the image and faces the camera;
    • it is not behind the observed depth by more than 3 cm, unless its pixel is its own;
    • it is not behind its pixel owner's surface (another SQ) by more than 5 mm;
    • it is not inside another SQ of its instance.
    Mean f of the visible targets is 0.47; 56 % have f < 0.5. f is used only in metrics so far.

5 · Surface sampling --sampling grid | cube

The surface points used by the Chamfer losses, the matching cost, the volumetric loss and the visibility (train.surface):

  • grid (default): a regular (η, ω) grid, n × n, as in SuperFlex. For boxy SQs the samples crowd onto edges and corners, and the inside of both pole faces gets no samples. Example: room_1 frame 130, wall SQ 0 has a point 146 cm from the nearest sample.
  • cube: a cube grid of 6 m² points (m = round(n / √6)), projected radially onto the SQ by s ⊙ u · F(u)−ε1/2. On box faces it is nearly uniform, its normals are analytic and it is differentiable in scale and shape. Same wall: 28 cm.

Sample counts: 16 × 16 for the losses, 8 × 8 for proposals, 4 × 4 in the matching cost. Metrics always use the grid, so they stay comparable with earlier runs.

6 · Losses

L = Lproposals + Ldecoder (+ the projection terms). Both main terms only see the visible targets (§ 3).

TermProposal loss (one-to-many, dense.losses)Decoder loss (Hungarian, train.losses)
PairingEach patch → its patch owner (§ 3). Up to 512 positive patches per frame, drawn ∝ 1 / (patches of their SQ).Hungarian matching of the 300 SQs to the visible targets. Cost = 10 · Chamfer (4 × 4) + 2 · (1 − pclass) + (1 − existence).
Existence2 · CenterNet focal loss against the heatmap2 · sigmoid focal loss (α 0.25, γ 2), matched = 1, normalized by the number of targets
Geometry10 · Chamfer + 10 · forced Chamfer (8 × 8)10 · Chamfer + 10 · forced Chamfer (16 × 16); --w_dec_cd scales both

Mask5 · BCE + 5 · Dice of the instance mask, against the share of the GT instance's pixels per patch

Forced Chamfer: the predicted SQ placed at the target's translation, as in SuperFlex, so that scale, shape and rotation learn while the position is still off.

Projection loss optional · render.py · --render_inst / _cls / _depth

Renderer. A differentiable ray caster in PyTorch, one ray per 2 × 2 pixels (84 × 150). Per (ray, SQ) pair: cull against the SQ's box, take 24 SDF samples and bisect to the entry point.

  • Silhouette: sigmoid(−SDF at the argmin / τ), with τ = half a pixel at that depth.
  • Depth: the entry depth with one Newton correction.
  • Compositing: front to back per ray, alpha = existence × silhouette.

It renders the final 300 SQs. Checks: 17 ms per frame (forward + backward); target SQs rendered at existence 1 match the GT instance image on 94 % of pixels; gradients match finite differences.

Terms, per ray:

  • inst: −log Σ w over the queries matched to the pixel's GT instance. It does not depend on how the instance is split into SQs.
  • cls: −log Σ w · p(class); no matching needed.
  • depth: relative L1 to the observed depth.

Status: first overfit ablation on 16 room_1 frames (f16s_*) running, results pending.

7 · Training

uv run python train.py --name <run> --topk --inputs dino --lr 3e-3 --warmup 0.05 --epochs 150 --eval_every 10
                       [--sampling cube] [--render_inst 1 --render_cls 1 --render_depth 1]
  • Optimizer. AdamW (weight decay 1e-4), OneCycle with 5 % warmup, batch 8, gradient clipping 1.0.
  • Speed. 0.18 s per step (0.45 s with the projection loss). A full run is 150 epochs, ≈ 1 h; the 16-frame overfit is 750 epochs, ≈ 25 min.
  • Queue. worker.sh pops queue.txt (two workers in parallel); logs go to results/<run>.log, metrics to results/<run>.json, checkpoints and test predictions to results/large_files/runs/, curves to trackio.

8 · Evaluation

On all test frames and 100 training frames, against the visible targets:

  • pair IoU (main metric): volumetric IoU of each Hungarian-matched pair, averaged. Also split by well-seen (f ≥ 0.5) and partly-visible targets.
  • dec_recall@0.5: share of targets with any decoder SQ at IoU > 0.5. enc_recall@0.5: the same for the 3150 proposals.
  • occupancy IoU of the union of SQs with existence > 0.5 against the targets' union; found: matched queries with existence > 0.5.
  • class accuracy of matched queries; mask coverage = best predicted-instance IoU per GT instance (patch level).
  • r_* (renderer): pixel instance and class accuracy of the rendered prediction, coverage, depth abs rel.

Removed from this overview

Still in the code as flags, but deprecated (tried, no gain or superseded); see the README for results.

  • DETR-style query model (model.Model, --patch_queries, --two_stage), its Tokenizer with point MLPs, and the dense model alone (--dense)
  • Sonata input (--inputs all/sonata; DINO alone is as good)
  • Union soft-IoU loss (--union), volumetric loss (--loss vol/both), SQ-level masks (--mask sq/sq_amodal)
  • Clipped Chamfer for partly visible targets (--partial clip, --clip_what, --vis_x, --forced_weight)
  • Training-set variants without gain: --min_patches, --dropout / --feat_drop. --train_frames diverse was never trained: every 10th frame already covers 98 % of what the diverse selection adds. --flip: its run never finished.
  • The smallest-SDF pixel owner (--owner_rule sdf, old caches in Thesis/data/backup)
